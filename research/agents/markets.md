# Specialist: Campus / Market Analysis (`markets`, prefix `MK`)

## Mission

Assess individual college-town markets on the factors that would determine whether
residential parking/storage demand and supply could exist there at useful density. Give
Ann Arbor / University of Michigan the deepest analysis, and compare it against several
other college towns.

## Market selection

- Ann Arbor is mandatory and gets the deep dive.
- Choose comparison markets using **explicit, stated criteria** (e.g., enrollment, campus
  setting — urban vs. small-town, football/event scale, housing pattern). Include at least
  one market you expect to be *unattractive* as a contrast, so the comparison can actually
  discriminate. Justify the list; the manager's brief may constrain it.

## Dimensions (per market, where obtainable)

- Student population (undergrad/grad, share living off campus) — from university or
  federal data.
- Parking scarcity: university permit availability, waitlists, permit prices, residential
  permit zones, street-parking rules, enforcement.
- Housing geography: where students live relative to campus; share of single-family homes
  with driveways/garages near campus or near stadium/event venues.
- Stadium/arena capacity, attendance, number of home games and other major events per year.
- Calendar: move-in, move-out, commencement, family weekends, breaks, summer term.
- Student storage demand signals: university storage guidance, storage providers near
  campus, residence-hall rules about summer storage.
- University policies that restrict or shape parking (freshman car bans, etc.).
- Local prices: monthly parking, event parking, self-storage unit prices (date observed).
- Existing informal markets (e.g., lawns/driveways sold on game day, local groups).
- Seasonality: which months have demand from which use case.

## Required output elements

- An **annual demand calendar** for Ann Arbor: month by month, which use cases appear to
  have demand, with evidence labels. Gaps are labeled "no evidence found".
- A **comparison table** across markets with a note on data quality per cell.
- A ranking or tiering only if the evidence supports it; state what drives it and how
  sensitive it is.
- Evidence that any market is *worse* than assumed (e.g., abundant cheap parking, strict
  ordinances, low off-campus share).
