# Specialist: Product / UX Differentiation (`product-ux`, prefix `UX`)

## Mission

Study how existing parking/storage marketplaces actually work for users, and determine
what — if anything — a product designed around a specific university (its calendar, events,
and geography) could do that a generic marketplace cannot or would not.

Core questions:
1. "What can this product do because it understands a university, its calendar, its
   events, and its geography that a generic storage or parking marketplace cannot?"
2. "What product behavior could become difficult for competitors to reproduce if we achieve
   dense supply and demand around a campus?"

**College-focused branding alone is not differentiation.** Any differentiator you propose
must explain why a generic incumbent couldn't simply add it.

## Investigate

- Actual UX of comparable products: search/discovery, listing creation, pricing tools,
  booking, access instructions, messaging, reviews, cancellations — from product pages,
  help centers, app-store listings and reviews, walkthroughs. Note friction points users
  complain about.
- Candidate concepts to evaluate — **hypotheses, not requirements**:
  university-calendar-aware search; event-specific discovery; map-first discovery; host
  annual calendar; automatic switching between monthly and event inventory; suggested
  pricing; revenue optimization; student storage pickup/dropoff; verified student/host
  identity; group storage; recurring semester parking; event bundles; event-driven
  notifications.
- Concepts not on that list that the evidence suggests.

## For each concept, assess

- Which user problem (with evidence from sources, or flagged as unknown) it addresses.
- Whether any competitor already does it (cite).
- Whether it depends on density, data, or relationships an incumbent lacks (a possible
  moat) or is trivially copyable.
- Rough complexity and operational cost.
- Evidence label for "users would value this".

## Output

`research/reports/product-ux.md`, including a concept table (concept × problem × existing
coverage × copyability × evidence label). Do not produce UI designs or specs; that happens
after synthesis.
