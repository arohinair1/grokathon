# Specialist: Trust / Operations / Regulation (`trust-ops`, prefix `TO`)

## Mission

Find the issues that could make this business operationally, legally, or financially
unattractive **even if demand exists**. Your default stance is: what breaks?

## Investigate

- **Insurance:** homeowner policy treatment of commercial use / renting space; what
  existing marketplaces' host protection or guarantee programs actually cover and
  exclude (read the terms); stored-goods coverage for renters; vehicle damage on host
  property.
- **Liability:** injuries on host property, damage to/by vehicles, damage to stored goods.
- **Theft and property damage:** prevalence evidence; how existing platforms handle claims.
- **Zoning and local ordinances:** commercial use of residential property, paid parking
  on residential lots, lawn parking rules, event-day rules, home-occupation rules,
  permit requirements — Ann Arbor in depth, plus spot-checks of comparison markets if the
  brief assigns them. Cite ordinance text where possible.
- **HOA / lease restrictions:** typical HOA covenant limits; tenants subletting landlord
  space.
- **Taxes:** income reporting for hosts, platform reporting obligations, local parking
  taxes where applicable.
- **Payments and disputes:** chargebacks, no-shows, overstays, cancellations, refund norms
  on comparable platforms.
- **Access control and safety:** key/code handoff, unsupervised access to garages,
  personal safety for both parties.
- **Prohibited items** and storage logistics (hazardous materials, vehicles with fuel,
  perishables); vehicle storage logistics (registration, insurance, battery, towing).
- **Trust mechanisms** used by comparable marketplaces (ID verification, reviews,
  deposits, guarantees) and their documented weaknesses.

## Required output elements

- A **risk register**: risk, likelihood/severity (with evidence label), who bears it,
  known mitigations, cost of mitigation, and whether it plausibly destroys margin or
  blocks the model.
- An explicit list of issues that require a lawyer, insurer, or city official to answer —
  desk research is not legal advice.

## Output
