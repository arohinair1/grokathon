# Specialist: Student / Renter Pain Points (`demand`, prefix `PP`)

## Mission

Find out how students, parents, visitors, commuters, and event attendees **currently**
solve parking and storage problems tied to the university calendar, how painful those
problems actually are, and what they pay. Determine whether the demand side of the
hypothesis is real, frequent, and underserved — or already handled well enough.

## Needs to investigate

- Summer storage; lease gaps (between leases, study abroad, internships).
- Semester storage.
- Vehicle storage (e.g., over summer, while abroad).
- Semester / monthly parking.
- Game-day parking.
- Graduation and family-weekend parking.
- Move-in / move-out logistics (staging, loading, short-term parking).

## For each need, establish

- Who has it (segment) and roughly when.
- Current solutions, ranked by how often they appear in evidence (including informal ones:
  friends, cars, landlords, leaving things at home, paying a storage company).
- What people pay, with date and location.
- Specific complaints and failure modes, quoted verbatim with sources.
- Whether people express willingness to use a stranger's garage/driveway — and stated
  concerns (trust, theft, access, distance).
- Signals of intensity: is this a recurring, painful problem or a minor annoyance?

## Sources

University resources (housing, parking, move-out guidance), student newspapers, Reddit
and other forums, reviews of storage/parking providers, parent forums/groups,
event-attendee discussions. Follow user-generated-evidence rules in `STANDARDS.md`
(count independent posts, date range, no personal details, no one-anecdote patterns).

## Be explicit about

- Needs that appear **well served** already.
- Needs that seem rare, low-value, or that people solve for free.
- What desk research cannot tell us and would need interviews/surveys — propose specific
  interview questions.

## Output

`research/reports/demand.md`.
